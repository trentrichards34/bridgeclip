# Direction: what makes these reels hold attention

These are the editing decisions behind the creator's reels. They apply to every style.

## Clarity first: one focus at a time on a full, building stage
- **The stage builds up.** Each stressed word adds one element or changes one element's state; earlier elements stay (moved aside,
  smaller) and keep moving. After 2-3 s the frame is full (3-6 elements), never a lone small object on empty paper.
- **Exactly one focus.** The newest element (the one that just landed or changed) is the focus: it is the biggest change on screen, it
  carries the accent colour, the presenter points at it and the camera leans toward it. Everything else is supporting cast. With the
  sound off the viewer still knows in half a second where to look.
- Big text: anything the viewer must read is ≥ 40 px on screen (UI text inside a phone/browser ≥ 28 px). Small mono labels, statuses
  and timestamps are texture and may be smaller. Max ~6 words of must-read text besides captions.
- Hold every readable state at least 0.4 s before it changes.
- Highlights (circles, boxes, underlines) must sit exactly on what they point at; check at full size.
- Full ≠ cluttered: supporting elements are quieter (no accent, smaller, already settled). If two things fight for attention, demote one.
- Style guides spell out the numbers (classic: `guides/styles/classic.md` → "Quality bar").

## Pace
- An event on every stressed word (≈ every **0.4-0.8 s**: an element enters or changes state); something big (slam, drop, cut, whip,
  wipe, topple, presenter move) **≥ 0.7 times per second**; nothing static for more than **1.2 s** (every landed element keeps an idle
  life: ticking, swinging, rolling, blinking, typing). Something small moves every **0.3-0.5 s**.
- Big moments land **on the stressed word** (the noun or number that carries the sentence), not before it.
- The first frame already shows something interesting: the hook visual is on screen at 0.0 s.
- No dead air: if the voice pauses, the picture still moves.
- Transitions: whip/swoop/wipe on beat changes, hard cuts inside a beat. Don't mix more than two transition types in one reel.

## Attention
- The eye follows motion, pointing and the brightest/most saturated thing. Make all of them land on the same thing: the focus being talked about.
- Presenter (classic style): points at the current focus almost all the time, re-aims when the focus moves, changes expression or gesture every phrase (wave on the hook, shock on a big number, hand over mouth on a fear, fists on a win). Once per reel he can BE the hero (steps to the centre, REC frame on his face). Docked bottom-right otherwise; content stays full size.
- Use the accent colour only for the key word / key object of the moment. If everything is orange, nothing is.

## Captions
- Captions follow the voice word by word. Emphasise 1 word per phrase (the stressed noun/number), never more than 2.
- Keep captions in the style's zone; never cover the hero object.

## Safe zones (Instagram / TikTok UI)
- Bottom ~300 px and right ~120 px are covered by the app UI. Keep key content above y ≈ 1400 and inside x 60..1020.

## Sound
- Voice is king; SFX are seasoning (mixed ~18 dB under the voice).
- One SFX per meaningful event: whoosh on transitions, pop on appearances, tick on counters, ding on success, thud/stamp on slams. No machine-gun clusters.
- Music is optional; most creators add a trending sound inside Instagram instead.

## Ending
- The CTA beat shows the keyword big and clear (a pill, sign or card), with a visual that says "comment" (comment bubble, pointing presenter). Hold the final frame ~0.5 s after the voice ends.
